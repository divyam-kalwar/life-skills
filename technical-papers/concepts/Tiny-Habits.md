# Tiny Habits

## 1. Tiny Habits - BJ Fogg

### Question 1
The most interesting idea for me was that we don't need to make big changes immediately. Even a very small action, like doing one push-up or flossing just one tooth, can help us build a habit. I liked this because starting small makes a habit feel less difficult, and it becomes easier to stay consistent.

## 2. Tiny Habits by BJ Fogg - Core Message

### Question 2
B = MAP means that a behaviour happens when three things come together:

- **M – Motivation:** How much I want to do the behaviour.
- **A – Ability:** How easy or difficult it is for me to do it.
- **P – Prompt:** A reminder or trigger that tells me to do it.

I can use this by making a new habit very easy, connecting it to something I already do, and using that existing routine as a reminder. This way, I don't have to depend only on motivation.

### Question 3
Celebrating after completing a habit helps create a positive feeling associated with that action. Even a small celebration, like saying “Well done” to myself or feeling proud, makes me more likely to repeat it. It helps me focus on my progress instead of how small the action was.

## 3. 1% Better Every Day Video

### Question 4
The most interesting idea for me was that small improvements made consistently can lead to big results over time. We often expect quick progress, but even improving a little every day can make a huge difference in the long run. It reminded me that consistency matters more than trying to do everything perfectly from the beginning.

## 4. Book Summary of Atomic Habits

### Question 5
The book explains that habits are connected to our identity. Instead of only focusing on what I want to achieve, I should focus on the kind of person I want to become. For example, instead of saying “I want to learn coding,” I can think of myself as someone who practises coding regularly. Every small action becomes evidence of that identity.

### Question 6
To make a habit easier, I can reduce the effort needed to start it. I can prepare everything in advance, keep the tools I need nearby, and make the habit small enough that I don't feel overwhelmed. For example, keeping my laptop ready and deciding what topic to practise makes it easier to start studying.

### Question 7
To make a habit harder, I can add friction or obstacles to it. I can remove distractions, keep my phone away, log out of distracting apps, or make it harder to access things that encourage the habit. This reduces the chances of doing it automatically.

## 5. Reflection

### Question 8
One habit I want to build is practising coding every day. I can start by solving just one small problem or studying for five minutes. I can connect it to an existing routine, such as starting after dinner. I will keep my laptop and notes ready so it is easy to begin. After completing it, I can celebrate the small achievement and gradually increase the time when I feel comfortable.

### Question 9
One habit I want to reduce is spending too much time scrolling on my phone. I can turn off unnecessary notifications, remove distracting apps from my home screen, and keep my phone away while studying. I can also set app limits and avoid using my phone when I sit down to work. This will make scrolling less convenient and help me use my time more productively.
